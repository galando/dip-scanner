What the table says, and what it does not:

  • The hold curve above is 38 signals from one month (June 2026), not the whole
    cache. A signal needs 21 sessions of data after it to have a 21-session
    forward return, so the most recent month of signals cannot appear at all.
    Read it as "this is what the sample we can measure did", not as a law.
  • The bounce-done rule costs money over these windows, monotonically. Every
    step up in min-hold improves the mean, and at 20 sessions the row is
    identical to switching the rule off (+6.10%, 124 trades). There is no
    interior optimum to find — the data's preferred setting is "off".
  • That saturation is the window ending, not the strategy topping out. A
    30-day run holds about 21 sessions, so a floor near 20 can barely fire.
    Anything past ~15 is untested here rather than better here.
  • RSI exit=75 and RSI exit=off are the same row: RSI never reaches 75 on a
    position this book holds, so 75 is "off" wearing a threshold.
  • take profit=15 and =20 are likewise the same row — above 15% the rule
    stops firing inside a month.
  • thesis break=3 buys the best worst-window in the table (+1.29%) at the cost
    of the worst mean (+3.91%) and only 4 of 8 windows beating SPY. It trades
    upside for downside; it is not free.
  • stop loss=10 moves almost nothing (+4.64% vs +4.53%): thesis-break reaches
    most losers first, so the stop rarely gets to act.

Adopted: min-hold=10 only. It is not the argmax of this table — 15 and 20 both
score higher — and it was not chosen by it. It is the largest floor that still
fires inside a 30-day run, so it stays under measurement instead of quietly
becoming "never sell on RSI", and it keeps a way out of a completed bounce for
holding periods this cache is too short to see. See src/validate.py for what
survives being tested off-sample: on the two windows that share no sessions the
95% interval on the improvement still includes zero, and the cache is too short
to support a walk-forward split whose halves do not share sessions.
